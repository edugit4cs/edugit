## Introduction

### What Is Artificial Intelligence?

Let’s get this straight right away:

- AI is not just about robots with red eyes taking over the world.
- AI is also not magic.
- AI is simply the ability of a computer or machine to mimic human intelligence:
  - learn from data;
  - solve problems;
  - make decisions; and
  - even learn from mistakes (just like you do!).

### AI in Pop Culture: Fun, But Fictional

Before diving deep, let’s debunk some fun AI myths:

| Myths | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| AI will become self-aware and rule the world. | Nope. AI today is task-specific and lacks emotions or goals. |
| AI can think like humans. | Not quite. AI processes data, but it doesn't "feel" or "think" like you. |
| Robots = AI. | Not always. Robots can exist without AI, and AI can exist without robots. |

### AI Is Already Around You

Believe it or not, AI is already a part of your daily life. Here are just a few examples:

| Everyday Activity | AI Involved |
| --- | --- |
| Asking Siri or Alexa | Natural Language Processing |
| Using Google Maps | Route optimization with Machine Learning |
| Streaming on Spotify | Music recommendations using Deep Learning |
| Seeing a Tesla drive itself | Computer Vision + AI decision-making |

So yes, every time you say “Hey Google” — you’re using AI.

### Why AI Matters to STEM Students Like You

No matter what you study — biology, physics, chemistry, engineering, or mathematics — AI is reshaping your field:

- Biologists use AI to detect diseases earlier.
- Chemists simulate molecules with AI before stepping into a lab.
- Engineers design smarter, safer cities using AI-based data analysis.
- Mathematicians model AI algorithms for faster computation.

Whether you're heading into medicine or robotics, AI is a tool you’ll want in your STEM toolbox.

## Overview and Objectives

### Overview

This module introduces foundational concepts in Artificial Intelligence (AI) for STEM students without prior computer science experience. Students will:

- Understand what AI is and why it matters in science and society.
- Learn the history of AI, key milestones, and its influence on modern life.
- Differentiate between AI, Machine Learning (ML), and Deep Learning (DL).
- Grasp the role of intelligent agents and Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).
- Explore ethical implications of AI.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. **Define** what Artificial Intelligence (AI) is and explain its role in modern society.
2. **Describe** the historical development of AI, from early ideas and myths to real-world technologies.
3. **Identify and explain** key terms and definitions used in AI, including algorithms, models, data, and intelligence.
4. **Differentiate** between Artificial Intelligence (AI), Machine Learning (ML), and Deep Learning (DL) with real-world examples.
5. **Explain** what intelligent agents are and how they interact with environments to solve problems.
6. **Discuss** the concept of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), how it differs from current AI systems, and its potential future uses.
7. **Evaluate** popular culture depictions of AI and compare them to real-world capabilities.
8. **Reflect on and discuss** ethical questions related to the development and use of AI in STEM fields.
